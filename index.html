Foothold

For students curious about startups whose background isn't a direct match: an AI agent that finds startups that fit you, checks whether they really hire new grads, and shows you a way in, based on your own resume.

Focus AI
v1 Nov 17
v2 Dec 1, project fair
01Users

Students curious about startups whose background isn't the obvious match, starting with economics and social science students at UChicago.

I'm the first user. I found YC and got lost in too many companies:

Researching one company took 20 to 30 minutes.
Many postings say "new grads ok", but not whether they really mean it.
I found the founders' LinkedIn and stopped there. I didn't know how to reach out, and my background isn't in their field.

It isn't just me. An a16z partner recently posted that most startup jobs are not posted on LinkedIn, and needed about 45 channels (VC job boards, aggregators, newsletters, fellowships) to say where they are.

From the other side: founders on r/startups say a new grad adds little for the first 6 to 12 months without close supervision. That is why "new grads ok" on a posting doesn't settle it.

Interview 1 of 3, done (text interview, November 3)

A classmate looking for marketing and sales roles at startups. In their words, translated from Chinese:

I look for marketing and sales roles on a startup job board, and judge whether my background matches by reading the job requirements. Finding the information is tedious and takes a long time.
I didn't reach out, because I wanted to spend the time first on judging whether the company and I fit. I got stuck at gathering the information for that judgment.
The most time-consuming step is understanding the company: the company itself, team members' emails, what the company has put on its public channels, and organizing all of it into my workflow.
It needs to understand my background, the company's background, and how to contact the team. Sending the email should still be controlled by a person.

What this changes: the blocker is before the first message, at judging fit, not after finding a founder. The resume profile, the company facts, and "who to contact" on each card are the right v1. Sending stays with the person, which v1 already does.

Interview 2 of 3, done (text interview, November 3)

A classmate who applies in volume. In their words, translated from Chinese:

I apply broadly: company websites I'm interested in, companies I've heard of, and timely posts on LinkedIn. I read the job requirements, but not in detail, and use them to modify my resume.
Finding a referral is a hassle, so I chose to mass-apply instead.
Preparing for interviews is the most annoying step. Sending applications isn't. Searching for information is acceptable.

What this changes: this is the opposite of what I expected. For a volume applicant, research time is not the problem, and interview prep is outside what Foothold does. The one thing they skip because it's a hassle is the referral, so "who to contact" and "your way in" are the only parts of v1 this person would use. The ranking and the fit judgment have to earn their place with the first interviewee's kind of user, not this one.

Interview 3: not done yet. One more classmate, 10 minutes, before the project starts on November 10. Their words will replace this box.

  • A MACSS student interested in startups who hasn't contacted any yet

What I ask, without describing Foothold: when did you last look into a startup, and why · how did you research it, and how long did it take · how did you judge whether they'd hire someone like you · did you think about contacting someone there, and did you · which step was the most annoying · if someone did this for you, which step would you hand over

What I expected to hear, now one for one: interview 1 confirmed that judging fit takes the time and blocks the first message; interview 2 did not, and says the hassle is finding a referral. Interview 3 decides which of the two is the common case.

02What v1 does

Upload your resume once. Get 10 startups that fit, each judged.

Step 1

Sign up

Upload your resume, pick a few interests

Step 2

Agent searches

YC companies and Hacker News "Who is hiring"

Step 3

10 cards

Ranked by fit, saved to your list

What each card shows
What they doIn plain words, tags explained
New grads?Yes / maybe / no, quoting the posting
Your way inQuoting a line from your resume and a fact about the company
Who to contactThe founders, and whoever posted the job on Hacker News (often a founder, with an email)

Later, not in v1: other channels, people to connect with beyond founders, a drafted first message. Never: scraping LinkedIn or Wellfound, or sending messages for you.

03Focus: AI

The hard part is judgment, not search.

Does this company really hire new grads, and how does my background fit? The app calls a model with tools to search and read postings, and three evals check how well it judges. I chose AI because I want to work on AI products, and building an agent I can measure is the skill I most want to practice.

80%+

New-grad judgment matches my labels on 40 postings

100%

Every quote exists in the posting or resume, so no made-up experience

90%+

Same company merged correctly across sources

05Stack

Built on what I used in Assignments 2 to 5.

Next.js · Vercel · Clerk

Each profile and saved list belongs to one account

Supabase + Cron worker

Stores companies and judgments; the Assignment 5 worker pulls new data

Gemini API, free tier

Company facts are judged once and shared; only "your way in" is per person

MCP and CLIs

Supabase MCP lets my coding agent check tables and worker logs while I build. Supabase, Clerk, Vercel, and GitHub CLIs for migrations, setup, deploys, and the repo

Data sources

v1: the YC directory (community JSON) and the HN Algolia API, both free. v2: each VC's portfolio page gives the company list, then the companies' public Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby job endpoints give the roles

Crawling, only where no API exists

Scrapling (Python) on a GitHub Actions schedule for VC portfolio pages, robots.txt respected, no stealth mode. Sites that forbid it get a link, not a crawler

06Ambition

Every channel where startups show up, in one place: VC job boards, aggregators, newsletters, and fellowships. Students use it free; startups pay for candidates who come with an honest, quoted reason why they fit.
